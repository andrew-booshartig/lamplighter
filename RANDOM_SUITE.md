# The Random suite: the plan, one stage at a time

Written 19 November 2026 by the session that did stage 1. The maker (19 November 2026): "when i click for another random selection, i don't want the page to reset to the top unless we reorganize the page so the passage is at the top. which is fine if the route to the random passage is its own section and not within the library selection. i think thats a fine structural decision being its importance. the more filters and clean methods for randomization the better. it makes the path less daunting if you can just explore freely. maybe it can be a fully featured, clean and modern randomization suite with many features."

Random is the flagship of the app. Three rules hold for every stage, and each stage's tests must keep them:
1. **Add power to Random, never remove it, and nothing that was visible may become hidden.** `tests/random-parity.test.js` is a table of 51 controls and abilities (eight rows for the Method section of stage 2), each counted in clicks; a new control gets a row, and no control may get further from the card. `tests/random-scroll.test.js` keeps Another from moving the page.
2. **Every draw replays.** A draw code (`<book>.<kind>.<seed>`) and the address of the page replay a passage exactly, for ever: a stage that changes how a passage is chosen from a seed must leave old codes replaying as they were drawn (the old lengths still do). The card's receipt ("How this was drawn") says in plain words how the draw was made.
3. **Neutral.** A way of drawing is a tool. The app never says a draw means anything, never says "your passage" or "a message for you", and describes the history of drawing lots from books (if it describes it at all) in plain words as something people have done, attributed, with a source opened first. The words already on the page ("The Lamplighter Project does not claim that a draw carries a message. A passage met by chance is often a good place to start thinking, and that is all this offers.") are the model.

## Stage 1: done (19 November 2026)

The card now stays until the new one is ready, is replaced in one step, and what the person was looking at stays on the screen (the card's top edge; the start of the new card when the person had scrolled into the old one; the controls when they had just used them). One template for every card, built only from data the app already holds: the religion the Atlas ties the book to as a chip that opens its page (religion › branch where the Atlas records a branch), with a small **+ Draw more** that adds that religion to the filters and draws; **+N** opens the rest when a book is tied to several; the religion of the Library shelf, or the family of the shelf, where the Atlas ties it to none (a family chip opens the family in All traditions), nothing where the app knows none; the kind of text in plain words ("a law code", "a gospel"); the period ("about 2nd c. BCE to 3rd c. CE"); the Library's edition credit ("Edition: Georg Bühler, 1886"); one plain sentence about the book (the first sentence of the catalogue's note); and the links **About this text** (the Library's source card, in a dialog) and **About this religion**. The credit parse (who and when) is a heuristic over free-form credits and falls back to the Library's own word, "Edition:".

## Where the later stages go (the room)

The page has two regions that later stages fill; nothing needs to be moved again.
- **The stage** (left, the card and what belongs to it): the card, its bar and action row, the Recent row, the parallels. A spread (stage 3) puts several cards here; the card's action row gets "Save as image" (stage 6) and "Wander" (stage 7).
- **The side** (right, under the card on a phone): the controls, as closed sections like Religions and Periods. Stage 2 adds a **Method** section next to "How to draw"; stage 4 adds filter sections next to Kinds and Shelves; stage 5 adds a quiet **Auto** control.
- **Tabs.** The place has one tab, Draw, and room for three more (at most four). A tab is added only when it has content, by adding an entry to `random.tabs` in the sections table (`tests/sections.test.js` updates in one line); the strip then appears by itself. Proposed: **Spreads** (stage 3: several cards on one screen is a different page), **History** (stage 5: Recent made lasting, the queue, Kept). Draw stays the first tab and the address `#/path/passage` stays its address.

## The stages

Sizes as in `BACKLOG.md`: S small (part of a session), M medium (about one session), L large. A stage is approved on its own, in any order after 1, except where it says it needs another.

### Stage 2: more ways to draw (the maker's "clean methods"), size M: DONE (21 November 2026)
**Design.** A **Method** section in the side with one choice and its input: **Chance** (what exists: the secure draw mixed with your own timing, the default), **A text** (type a word, a question or a name), **A date** (any date, the birthday or one's own), **Dice** (virtual or your own). The daily draw that exists (a saved filter with "daily") stays and is listed here as a fifth, "Today". Each draw method makes the same 128-bit seed the page already uses, so the card, the receipt and the draw code work unchanged; only the way the seed is made differs.
- **A text:** the seed is SHA-256 of "lamplighter-text|" + the text, normalised (Unicode NFKC, lower case, spaces collapsed). The same text with the same filters gives the same passage on any device; the address carries it (`?seed=…` with the filters), so it can be shared. The receipt says: "Drawn from the text you typed as a seed. The same text gives the same passage." and that the text's meaning played no part.
- **A date:** the same, from "lamplighter-date|2026-11-19" (the daily draw already does exactly this with a saved filter's name). The date is typed or picked; nothing is stored unless it is saved with a saved filter.
- **Dice:** the numbers choose the passage from the filtered pool and are shown. Design: the pool is listed in a fixed order (shelf, then book, as the Books section lists it); rolls are read as digits of a number in base 6, 10 or 20 (d6, d10 or d20, "two d6", "a percentile"), enough of them to cover the pool; a number past the end of the pool is thrown out and rolled again (rejection, the same step the secure draw uses, so no book is likelier); a last percentile roll says how far through the book the place is. The dice are the secure generator's numbers, or the person's own typed in from real dice; either way the receipt shows the numbers and the page explains the mapping in three lines, and the same numbers give the same passage. A draw code for dice carries the numbers.
**Data.** None new. `sha256`, the seed and stream functions (`01-random.js`), the daily draw (`psvDailySeed`) and the pool order (`psfPool`) exist. The new code is the Method section, the three seed makers (pure, in `2z-passage-core.js`) and their receipts.
**Neutrality.** The risk is a person reading an answer into the result, most of all with a typed question. The page says plainly that the passage is chosen by arithmetic from the text's letters, not by its meaning, and does not answer it; no "your passage" or "for you"; a question is never repeated back in the card. The date method says nothing about the person's birth or the date's significance, and no astrology or numerology wording (the Calendars and the sky pages grade those claims; this page makes none). Dice show every number and the mapping, so nothing is hidden. If the history of opening a book at random for guidance is described (the *sortes Virgilianae* with Virgil's *Aeneid* in Roman and later European use, the *sortes sanctorum* with the Bible, the *fāl* taken from the Divan of Hafez in Persian-speaking lands), it is in plain words, attributed to the people who did it, each statement labelled documented or reported, and each with a source opened first: none is written from memory.
**Tests.** Same text gives the same code; different texts give different codes; the mapping covers every book of a pool equally (chi-square, as `random.test.js` does); dice numbers replay; the three seeds are 32 hex characters; parity rows for the four methods; the page does not move on any of them.

**What was built (21 November 2026).** A closed **Method** section beside How to draw, five ways, one panel at a time: **Chance** (a button that draws like Another; Another, Space and N are untouched and are still what draws by chance), **A text**, **A date**, **Dice** (d6, d10 or d20; "Roll for me" with the secure generator, or the numbers of the person's own dice typed in; Enter works in each box) and **Today** (the saved filters marked Daily, one button each; "Show today's" stays on the saved filter). Each makes the same 128-bit seed, so the card, its receipt and its draw code work unchanged. Code: the pure `psm*` functions in `src/js/2z-passage-core.js` (`psmTextSeed`, `psmDateSeed`, `psmDay`, the dice: `psmNeed`, `psmBook`, `psmRoll`, `psmTyped`, `psmParseFaces`, `psmDiceSeed`, `psmDiceFromSeed`, `psmPlaceOf`, `psmOrder`, and `psmHow`, the receipts' words); `psxMethodHTML` and `psxMethodPatch` in `src/js/6d2-passage-filters.js`; `draw({ method })`, `psgStreamOf` and a one-line hook in `psgBelow` in `src/js/6d-passage.js`; `.psgx-mx*` in `src/css/app.css`. Tests: `tests/random-methods.test.js` (125; mutation-checked), 8 parity rows (51 in all), 9 scroll checks (a text, a date, dice rolled and dice typed, each pressed at the controls with a shorter and a taller card, and Another after a text), and one shared-test regex widened (`path-render`: the pick of a page may be made with `psgStreamOf`, which is `randStream` for every seed that is not dice).
- **A text** and **A date**: SHA-256 of `lamplighter-text|` + the text (NFKC, lower case, spaces collapsed) and of `lamplighter-date|YYYY-MM-DD`. The typed text is read once and never kept, sent, written in the address or the history, or repeated in the card; the address carries the draw code (its last part is the seed) and `meth=text|date`, so the receipt's words survive a reload or a link. The date is likewise not kept. Both draw once with no no-repeat redraw (the same input must give the same passage), and replay by their code.
- **Dice.** The list is the books the sections leave, shelf by shelf (`psmOrder`: the collection's number, then the Library's order). As many dice as it takes to pass the end of the list are one number in base 6, 10 or 20 (a d10's 10 counts as 0); that number divided by the length of the list leaves the book's place, and the numbers at the very top are thrown out and rolled again, so over every reading of the dice each book is named exactly as often (tested over all readings, and by chi-square on the secure generator). Two more dice say how far through the book: `psgBelow` takes its first place from the dice (`rng.at`, set only for a dice seed) and everything after from the seed's own stream. The seed **holds the numbers** (`d1ce`, die, how many name the book, those digits, the two of the place), so the draw code carries them and the receipt reads them back out of a replayed code.
- **Decisions made for the maker (easy to change):** (1) *rejection:* the design said "a number past the end of the pool is thrown out"; the page uses the secure draw's own step instead (the number is divided by the list's length, and only the few numbers at the top are thrown out), because with three d10 on 197 books the literal rule would throw out four rolls in five; each is exactly unbiased; the page explains it in three lines (under half of the readings are ever thrown out, and on the default list of 197 books 8% for a d6 and 0.5% for a d10 or a d20). (2) *Another is still chance:* a way draws once when it is asked, and Another, Space and N after it draw by chance again (the line beside Another says so), so the same text cannot be asked for "another"; making Another continue the chosen way is a decision, not a default. (3) *flat list:* with dice every book has the same chance (a shelf of 66 Bible books comes up 66 times as often as a shelf of one), unlike chance, which gives every shelf the same chance first; the page says so. (4) *a saved mix* is not used by dice (the list is what the sections show). (5) *the percentile is literal at the section level:* the first place of the first try is where the dice say (a book of 100 equal chapters is entered at the chapter that many hundredths in); inside a chapter, and on a retry after a note or a list is passed over, the seed's own stream decides. (6) *The daily draw's receipt* said "secure random" (the draw was never random); it now says what it is.
- **Left / for the maker:** a saved filter that remembers a method (a date or a dice list) is not built, and nothing about a date is stored anywhere; the history of drawing lots from books, which the neutrality paragraph above allows only with a source opened first for every sentence, is not written (nothing from memory), so it waits for the maker's go-ahead and a source list; Today lists only saved filters marked Daily (making one is "+ Save this filter" with Daily ticked; no shortcut was built); dice read at most the pool the sections leave (no book weighting); a die with another number of faces (d4, d8, d12) and a pair read as 2–12 are not offered; the second place of a retry is not placed by the dice.

### Stage 3: spreads, size M to L
**Design.** A **Spreads** tab: **a spread of 3 or 7** (several draws at once from the same filters, never the same book twice, each a small card with reference, a few lines and Open), and **one passage from each of several chosen traditions on one card** for comparing: the traditions are ticked in the Religions tree (the same tree as the Draw page, kept in the address), the draw takes one passage from each with the same method, the order is alphabetical or random but never by size or by any ranking, and every column has the same template (reference, words, frame line). The spread can be kept as a **Passage set** (Notebook, which already puts passages side by side), clipped to the notebook, or copied with all its citations. Another redraws all of it, or one column (a press on that column's own Another).
**Data.** None new: the pool and the tradition filters (`psfPool`, `psfTree`), the Passage sets (`THREADS`), `NB.clip`, `provCite` for the citations. The draw code of a spread is the list of its cards' codes.
**Neutrality.** Putting passages from different traditions side by side invites a verdict ("this one says it better"). The cards use one template, equal room, no highlighting and no order that says rank; the page says nothing about how the passages relate beyond "drawn separately from each"; a tradition with no passage under the current filters says so and is not skipped silently (the same rule as an empty part of a mix); the number per tradition is always the same. The Compare tab's rule applies: a likeness between two passages is a place to start reading, not a claim that they are connected.
**Tests.** Each column satisfies the filters; no repeated book; every chosen tradition appears; the same codes replay; the strip shows two tabs, Draw first; the page does not move when one column redraws.

### Stage 4: more filters, size M (four parts, each can be approved alone)
**4a. Simple flags (S).** *Verse or prose* (whether the text numbers its verses, as the Bible texts do, or runs in paragraphs: `psgIsVerseText`); *with or without commentary* (a switch that lets the annotated editions in, or keeps them out as now: the lists `PSU_ANNOTATED`, `PSU_ABOUT` in `2z-passage-pool.js`). These are book-level facts that exist; the filters become sections beside Kinds, with counts and the same dimming as the others. **Data:** none new. **Neutrality:** none beyond the labels: "verse" and "prose" describe the typesetting, not the quality.
**4b. A word or phrase the passage must contain (M).** The draw is random among the search results: the page searches the books the other filters leave (usually 20 to 200 of the 1,400), text by text, as the Search page does, shows "searching 120 books, 340 places so far", then picks uniformly among the places found, and says how many places and how many books it chose from. The word is searched as it is typed, with the same rules as the Search page (case, diacritics, whole word). **Data:** the search core (`2r-search-core.js`) and its streaming; no index. A broad filter is slow (the texts are 1.7 GB), so the section says "Narrow the books first for a quick answer" and the draw can be stopped; a later step (L) is a small word-to-book index built by a tool, as `passage_index.js` builds the Atlas links. **Neutrality:** the passage found is a passage that uses the word; the card says "contains the word X" and not "is about X". A word can be loaded in either direction (a loaded term chosen to get a loaded passage): the filter is the person's own and is shown on the card, like any other filter, and the page does not suggest words.
**4c. A theme (S to M).** The app already has motifs (`2x-motifs.js`: stories, beliefs, rites, ways of living, each a hand-written list of words) and themes in the Compare tab. A theme filter draws a passage tagged with the chosen motif, by the same tags the Motifs page uses. It is found by words, and the card says so ("found by words, not certainly about it"), as the Motifs page does. **Data:** exists: `similar/motifs.js` (195 KB, built 17 November 2026) lists the tagged passages of the texts, and `6v-motifs.js` already reads it without the big similarity index; no new tool and no slow scan. The one decision is whether a theme draws uniformly among the tagged passages or, as every other draw does, shelf first, then book (the second keeps one big book from crowding out the rest, and is the recommendation). **Neutrality:** the motif list is the project's own hand-written list, crude on purpose, and the label keeps saying so; every motif is offered in the same way, with no tradition's themes put first.
**4d. Has a parallel (S to M).** Draw only from the passages that have a known parallel: either the curated parallel sets (`2q-data-parallels.js`, about 26 sets, a small pool, each item found at run time by its opening phrase) or any passage with a close match in the Find similar index (`similar/`, 59 MB, 296,059 passages, exists). The first is small and exact; the second is large and statistical, and the card says which it is. **Neutrality:** "has a parallel" means "appears in a set people have long set side by side" (curated) or "shares words and motifs with another passage" (statistical): the relation is never called borrowing or influence (the Parallel passages page grades relations for that reason, and the card keeps to it).
**Tests (all of 4):** each filter narrows the pool and its count is right; zero is dimmed with a reason; the address carries it; saved filters keep it; old addresses unchanged.

### Stage 5: explore freely, size M
**Design.** (1) **Auto**: a quiet control in the side, "Draw one every [10 | 30 | 60] seconds", with Pause; off by default; stops when the page is left, when a dialog opens, when the person types, and never starts by itself; respects reduced motion (no fade between cards) and never plays sound; a pressed Pause keeps the passage on screen. (2) **Swipe** on a phone: left for the next card, right for Previous (through what was drawn), the same as the arrow keys; a swipe that starts inside the card's text selection, or a horizontal scroll of a table, is left alone. (3) **History**: the Recent row made lasting, kept on this device (the last 200 draws with their codes, never sent anywhere, carried by Setup's Export all like the rest), as a **History** tab with the Recent draws, a **queue** ("Read later": add the card, take the next from the queue, reorder or drop) and the Kept passages that exist, every entry replaying by its code.
**Data.** None new: `PSG_SESSION` (now in memory), `passage.kept` (exists), a new `passage.history` and `passage.queue` in the same store.
**Neutrality.** Auto plays nothing and says nothing; it draws more, and nothing else. History is the person's own record on the device; the page does not summarise it ("you read mostly…") or rank traditions by what was drawn: counting would invite a story about the person that the app does not tell. The reading statistics on Highlights and notes follow the same rule.
**Tests.** The timer stops on leave, on pause and on a dialog; the swipe moves through history; the history is capped and escaped; Export all carries it; the page does not move on an auto draw unless the person is looking at the card.

### Stage 6: save the card, size S to M
**Design.** In the card's action row: **Save as image** (a PNG drawn on a canvas: the reference, the words, the frame line, the citation with its source and licence, the draw code, and "The Lamplighter Project"; the text wraps at a readable width in the reading settings' type) and **Print or save as PDF** (a print stylesheet that shows only the card with its citation, so the browser's own print dialog makes the PDF with any font the passage needs). Both are files made on the device and given to the person with the app's own save step (`download()`); nothing is sent.
**Data.** None new: the card has the citation (`citeText`, `provCite`), the licence and the source line from the catalogue. A hand-built PDF is not planned: it needs the fonts of every script the English texts carry, which would add a large font file; the print dialog avoids that.
**Neutrality and rights.** Only public-domain or openly licensed texts are drawn, and the image says so on its face (the edition, its licence, its source), so a shared picture carries its credit and cannot be mistaken for a quotation of a modern book. The image says "drawn at random" and gives the code; it carries no caption that interprets the passage. A passage from a machine-read scan carries the scan note ("Machine-read from a scan: some words may be wrong").
**Tests.** The image function is given the card's fields and returns a canvas of the right size (a stub canvas in the tests); the citation is in it; the print stylesheet hides everything but the card; escaped text.

### Stage 7: Wander joined to Random, size M
**Design.** On the card, **Wander** (next to Parallels): the page offers the closest passage in each of a few other traditions (the Find similar statistics the Wander page already uses), one for each; choosing one makes it the next card, and a thin **trail** under the card shows the chain (passage, passage, passage), each step a link back, dated, and the trail can be kept as a Passage set. The Wander page stays under Library → More → Compare passages; this is the same engine reached from the card, and "Another" leaves the trail.
**Data.** The Find similar index (`similar/`, 59 MB, exists), `6x-wander.js` and `2w-similar-core.js`. Needs stage 3's small cards if the offered passages are shown as a row.
**Neutrality.** The Wander page's own sentence is kept on the card: "a hop is a place to start reading, not a claim that the two are connected". One offered passage per tradition, the same template, no "best match" and no score shown as a verdict.
**Tests.** A hop lands on a card with a code that replays; the trail is kept in the address or the session; the page does not move on a hop.

## Words to use and to leave out (neutral)
Use: "drawn", "chosen by arithmetic", "the same text gives the same passage", "a place to start reading", "found by words", "documented", "reported", "disputed". Leave out: "your passage", "a message", "meant", "fate", "luck", "answer" (for a typed question), "the best parallel", a ranking of traditions, a count of what a person has drawn presented as a pattern about them. When a history of drawing lots from books is told, say who did it and where, and open a source for every sentence before writing it.
