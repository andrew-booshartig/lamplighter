## Rules that keep it from getting messy

- **One shape for every new kind of record**, the one Stories and Rites already use: id, name, tradition, place and coordinates, year, how the date is known, an evidence grade (attested, probable, debated, popular story, mixed), a plain "how sure are we", links into the Library and Atlas, and a care note where one is needed. One data file (`2?-data-*.js`), one view file (`6?-*.js`), one test file for each kind.
- **The map gets layers, not new pages.** Sound, image and substance records become kinds of event in the Atlas, so the flat map, the time-lapse and the calendars work on them with no new code.
- **A new top-level group only when it has four views.** Connections already holds eight views (the most a group should hold), so new connection pages cannot go there. Proposed group when ready: **Beyond text** with Sound, Image, Substances and Hidden currents. Until two of the four exist, substances sit in Practice → Rites (the "sacred drinks and plants" card is already there) and the Jewish strand in Connections is not extended further.
- **Fiction is its own shelf**, labelled as imagined, never mixed into a graded page.
- **Rights.** Only public-domain or CC0 audio and images, or sound and pictures the app makes itself (notated rhythms played by the app's own synth; sefirot, mandalas and calendars drawn as SVG). What the app draws or plays itself stays generic (it does not imitate a particular living tradition’s sacred objects or recordings); the Library’s texts and pictures follow only the rights rule, and nothing is left out for being sacred, secret or sensitive (the earlier line “restricted material stays out” was never the maker’s and was withdrawn on 17 November 2026).
- **Chemistry** is history, botany and pharmacology with grades, never doses, preparations or encouragement; hard cases carry the same "understood, not endorsed" note as the hard history.

## The reader: what the maker asked for next (15 November 2026)

Today's reader (`#/path/read/<id>`) already has highlights in colours, notes, paragraph and section bookmarks, reading progress, find, text size, English-only and **My marks**, and **My marks** can be exported as Markdown, but the reader shows **one section at a time**, has no pages, no reading themes, and a text itself cannot be saved, exported or printed as a whole. Asked for: a legitimate reader where an entire text can be pulled up and marked by page, parts saved with highlights and notes, and whole texts exported or saved for e-readers and for printing; reading themes; and a new name for the reader that is non-denominational and meaningful to everyone. Built on 15 November 2026: reading themes and type settings (the Aa panel: nine themes, four faces, size to 40 px, spacing, width, alignment, verse numbers, Easy reading); (1) the whole-text view with virtual pages, a page bar and a slider; (3) export of any text or Bible version as an EPUB, plain text, Markdown, a web page or a print-ready page, with your highlights and notes if you want them. Still to build: a paged view with real page turns, (2) reading themes and typography, (4) the new name, a page-bookmark list. The original proposal, in order: (1) a whole-book view (continuous scroll with a table of contents, built in chunks so a 15 MB text stays smooth) and a paged view with real page numbers and a page bookmark; (2) reading themes and typography (paper, night, sepia, high contrast; serif, sans and a dyslexia-friendly face; size, line width, spacing, verse numbers on or off); (3) export: EPUB (valid EPUB 3 with contents, for any e-reader), plain text and Markdown, print-ready pages (a print style sheet with running heads and page numbers, so the browser's Save as PDF works), and export of your highlights and notes (Markdown, and a printable page); (4) a name; (5) the Bibles side by side opened from the reader. The text and the user's marks stay in the browser; nothing is uploaded.

## The reader is not yet a professional reader app (the maker's verdict, 15 November 2026)

What I measured in the live app: on a 1024×768 screen the first line of a book starts 781 px down the page, below the fold, because the page's own header, search box and tabs (about 380 px) and the book's header card (about 400 px: title, progress, section controls, find, six buttons, contents, marks line) sit above it; on a phone-width screen it starts about 1,370 px down. Professional reader apps open on the text. Gaps, in the order I would close them (built on 15 November 2026: 1, the marks sheet of 4 and the find box of 5 in the slim bar's sheet; then, the same day, 2 the paged view, 3 progress and time left, 5 the search panel and keyboard, 6 listen, 7 look up, and the part of 10 that needs no person: focus, keys, reduced motion, labels and theme colours. Not built: 8 and 9. Not done: the real-device and screen-reader checks in 10):
1. **Open on the text.** Immersive reading: hide the app's sidebar and header while reading, collapse the book header into one slim bar (title, progress, Aa, Save, contents, search, marks) that shows on tap or scroll-up and hides on scroll-down.
2. **(Built.) A paged view** with page turns (tap zones, swipe, arrow keys, Space and Page Down), page numbers in the margin, and a margin ribbon for page bookmarks; the scroll view stays.
3. **(Built.) Reading progress that means something:** percent through the book, pages left, an honest time-left estimate from your own pace, "continue reading" on the Library shelf.
4. **A marks panel inside the reader:** all highlights, notes and bookmarks of this text in a side sheet (jump, edit, delete, filter by colour), not only on the separate My marks page.
5. **(Built.) Search in the book** as a panel with every match in context and next/previous, and keyboard shortcuts (/ to search, g to go to page, j/k, n/p) in both views.
6. **(Built; voices not heard.) Listen:** read aloud with the voices already on the device (the app already speaks guided meditations), with a sleep timer.
7. **(Built.) Look up:** select a word to see the glossary entry, or search every text for it (search and similar already exist from a selection).
8. **(Not built.)** The texts carry no structured notes to attach popovers to (they were cleaned of footnotes; the Bibles page has its own notes). If notes are added to a text later, popovers come with them. **Footnote-style popovers** for notes, a quiet "Notes" tray, and note editing without a modal.
9. **(Not built; belongs with hosting.) Offline install** (a manifest and service worker, from the distribution plan) so it opens like an app.
10. **(Partly built.) Accessibility pass:** done without a person: focus goes into the sheet and returns, Tab stays inside it, the menu works with arrow keys, reduced motion switches the page slide and fades off, every button is labelled, each theme colours the paged view's footer and end card so a light theme in a dark app leaves no pale text on a pale page, and the new marks carry dark text. ## Word help: a dictionary for the texts’ own words (16 November 2026)

## The built-in dictionary: Webster’s 1913 (16 November 2026)

**Asked for and approved.** After the Word help work I said the glossary covers only religious words and offered a public-domain offline dictionary for the plain ones (apostleship, tardy, kine, thou); I named Webster’s 1913 from Project Gutenberg (eBook #29765, 28.9 MB) and asked before fetching it. The maker said “Yes, fetch it”. Only that file was fetched.

**Built.** `tools/build_dictionary.py` turns the book into `dictionary/` (113,321 entries in 19 script files of about 1.1 MB, 19.7 MiB, loaded one file at a time like the texts), `2z-dict-core.js` finds a word (the key, the file, the forms to try, the pointers, the care flag, the markup), `6zx-dictionary.js` loads the files and writes the section, and the section appears in four places: Look up (and the card’s “Look further”), the Glossary page, the search box (“Define …”), and nowhere uninvited (the reader does not underline ordinary words: the dictionary is for a word you select). It is the fifth saved library in Freedom → Take it with you, in the website folder, in the zips with the texts, and a pack zip of its own. Tests: `tests/dictionary.test.js` (111 checks), and the offline, packs and privacy tests were extended.

**What it had to solve.** The book prints one entry in several shapes, so the parser reads it line by line, and the first version left the subject label alone as a meaning in 7,668 entries (“(Anat.)” and then the definition in the next part): found by reading samples, fixed, and held by a test. Quotations had to be cut without cutting definitions, which needed a list of the 737 authors’ names found by counting what ends a quotation (the poet Pope was on a list of plain words and so left his name on 1,015 meanings until it was fixed). The forms of old English (shouldst, mayest, goeth, begat) are found by rules and, for irregular ones, from the lists in the head lines; Webster’s own “imp. & p. p. of Begin” entries are written out in words. An obsolete entry is listed after the living one with the same spelling (thee, the pronoun, before thee, to thrive).

**Left out on purpose, and why.** The word histories (the Gutenberg text lost the Greek and most of the Old English letters, so many are cut off or wrong), the quotations and their authors’ names, long usage notes and the discussions under “Syn.”, and the pronunciation marks. Say if any should come back.

**Judgement calls for the maker.** (1) 570 entries are flagged for care because they use the words of 1913 about peoples and faiths; the flag is a word list of mine, not reviewed by anyone. (2) The box says once, for a flagged word, that the entry is shown to be understood, not endorsed, and always says it is a book of its time. (3) The dictionary is an optional pack: the app-only and with-map zips leave it out, and say so in the box where it is missing. (4) It is 20 MB; it could be trimmed to the commonest forty thousand headwords if size matters more than completeness.

**Not done, and said so.** Nobody read the entries; the data was checked by tests and by reading about a hundred entries, and the faults I found and left are in FACT_CHECK.md. Words newer than 1913 are missing (WordNet or a modern dictionary would add them; neither was fetched), and most names. Nothing was tried on a phone, a screen reader, Safari or Firefox; the section was checked in the desktop browser pane at desktop and phone width.

## Toward a complete research suite on god and spirituality (18 November 2026; the maker's question, with cults, fiction and wars between sects in mind)

**Stance confirmed by the maker, 18 November 2026: "stay as neutral as possible. i'm not building this to direct opinions. its only a tool for people to form their own opinions."** Written down as follows.

**Stance first (a page, not code):** neutral and scholarly, every claim graded, "understood, not endorsed"; living people and groups only from documented sources (court findings, government reports, major investigations), labelled alleged where not proven; no verdict on which side is right; violence shown beside peace and coexistence.

**Structural gaps:** (1) one data model for every kind of thing: today the Atlas holds traditions, places, interactions, events and texts; add first-class people, events and conflicts, concepts, controversies, works (fiction) and organisations, each with sources, dates, places, a grade and relations, so a new domain is a data file and a view, not code; (2) a data-driven Library (a text is dropped files, not a line in `LIB_RAW`); (3) a list-don't-host bibliography layer for what cannot be hosted (post-1930 scholarship and fiction: where to borrow, cited and graded); (4) suite tools: one search across everything, citation export (BibTeX, RIS), research collections, filtered timelines; (5) a search index so the web version is usable.

**Content domains, in order of value:** (1) conflict and peace (wars, persecutions, schisms, inquisitions, massacres, forced conversions, pogroms, reform, treaties, dialogue, and doctrinal disputes such as Arian, filioque, Sunni and Shia): each with parties, place, dates, causes graded religious, political, economic or mixed (scholars dispute "wars of religion"), casualty ranges with sources, primary texts from each side; a new layer on the Atlas map and timeline; start with a pilot of about 50 events across five traditions; (2) concepts and arguments (what "god" means: mono-, poly-, pan-, deism, the apophatic; afterlife, sin, salvation; arguments for and against; theodicy) and non-belief (atheism, agnosticism, freethought, humanism, the critics from Lucretius to Nietzsche); (3) people (founders, prophets, saints, mystics, reformers, critics) from Wikidata (CC0, fetched at build time with approval) plus hand-written entries for the key ones; (4) high-control groups (started): add a framework (Lifton, Hassan, Singer, and the academic new-religious-movements view, neither a verdict), cases from records, how to assess a group, exit and recovery help; (5) religion in fiction: the public-domain shelf exists (Dante, Milton, Bunyan, Lovecraft) plus a catalogue, not texts, of modern works and invented religions (Discordianism, Pastafarianism, Jediism) with themes and where to read them; (6) science and society of religion (anthropology, psychology, cognitive science, sociology) as graded summaries with citations and open datasets (Pew, World Values Survey, Our World in Data); (7) finish what is started: sacred places and pilgrimage, the art gallery with real works, music.

**Risks:** defamation (living groups), rights (modern scholarship and fiction), bias, graphic violence and hate polemic (label and contextualise, "explain, don't remove"), contemporary conflicts (scholarly consensus and several perspectives only), scope creep (write the scope down).

**Order:** stance page; data model and data-driven Library; conflict pilot to prove the model; concepts and non-belief; people; finish high-control groups; fiction catalogue; science of religion; polish and web.

## The quality pass: proofreading the whole collection and choosing the best translations (18 November 2026; the maker's request: do it once the collection is complete)
The maker's question: "can we do the massive undertaking of proofreading the entire database after we have our collection absolutely complete, then cross reference to make sure we have the best translations possible for everything?" The answer is yes, in that order, so that no work is done twice. It came from a Random card out of *The Hermetic Museum* (`h-museum`, a Digital Library of India scan of Waite 1893): "z¢s earth" for "the earth" and "ove thing" for "one thing" are the scan's reading, not the translation, and nothing on the card said so (the card now carries a note for machine-read scans; see `BACKLOG.md`, "Scan quality").

**Q0, measure (automatic; no model reads the books).** A score for every text from its full text: typed copy or machine-read scan (the licence and note say which), the noise rate (tokens that are not words, symbols inside words, long-s patterns: the Random gate's tests, `src/js/2z-passage-core.js` and `tools/passage_words.js`), the share that is not English, length, whether it has printed page marks. Output `TEXT_QUALITY.csv`, worst first. The same pass builds the skeleton of the works registry `WORKS.csv`: which Library texts are editions or translations of one work (the Find similar index already finds duplicates; `tools/similar_skip.json` names many).
**Q1, better sources (the long pole).** For every text over a noise line, look for a cleaner source of the same edition: a better scan with better OCR (archive.org usually holds several), or a proofread typed copy (Project Gutenberg, Wikisource, Sacred Texts, eBible, SuttaCentral, Perseus). Swap only when it is the same edition and keeps its printed page numbers and sections (`lamplighter-original-page-references`: never guess). The Global Grey re-sourcing (82 of 94) is the model. In batches of 25 to 50, ordered by how much a text is read (Random's pools, the stations' study shelves, the Bibles, the most opened); the first in the queue is `h-museum`. Ask first before every batch (the files, the sources, the size).
**Q2, repair what is left, openly.** A corrections layer beside `texts/`, never inside it: a reviewed list per text of exact corrections (bad string to corrected string) found by a tool (a non-word with one near neighbour, a symbol inside a word, known confusions), applied when the text is read, shown with a dotted underline and the scan's own reading in the tooltip, with a switch in the reader ("Show the scan's own reading"). Each correction is reviewed before it enters the list; nothing changes silently; the page scan still matches the text.
**Q3, proofread by sampling, then by reading.** For each text, read a sample of passages (about 20) against the page scan to measure the real error rate; a text above the line goes back to Q1 or is queued; the most-read texts are proofread through against the scan. A "report a wrong word" action in the reader that builds a list the person can send is a small addition.
**Q4, the best translations.** The registry `WORKS.csv`: each work, and under it every edition in the Library (translator, year, original language, method literal or literary, complete or partial, known problems, rights, the Q0 score). A recommended edition per work by criteria written out (complete, readable, faithful as scholars judge, clean text, public domain), with the reason and the maker able to overrule. Where the best translation is modern and in copyright it is listed (author, publisher, year), not hosted, and the card says why the hosted one is older. A neutral "Which translation?" card per work, graded documented, reported or disputed, in the style of the High-control groups cards (for example the standard public-domain set of the Sacred Books of the East is archaic and sometimes softened: say it as scholars say it). In the reader: "Other translations of this work" with a side-by-side view (the Bibles side by side are the model).
**Q5, cross-reference and publish.** Every work has a recommended edition; every duplicate is explained; no orphan; a short `QUALITY.md` per shelf; the numbers in the README; the final integration.

**Cost.** Q0 is hours of computation and one session. Q1 and Q4 are the long poles: a research session per batch or per shelf (17 shelves), run one or two at a time inside the usage windows, so weeks at the current pace rather than days. Q2 and Q3 run beside them. Rules throughout: ask first before each download batch; public-domain or openly licensed text only; never guess a correction; keep sections and printed page numbers; "recommended" is a labelled editorial choice with its reason, never a verdict.
